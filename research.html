peer-reviewed and accepted

restored lines: a model finds the poet's hardest line breaks and declines to write them

2026 · sole author · accepted, neurips 2026 creative ai · pdf · openreview · code

a line break is the smallest unit of poetic decision. nothing in the sentence about the red wheelbarrow requires anyone to break it after "wheel". williams did that. this strips the breaks from 2,059 public-domain poems, grades every break by how much dependency structure it severs, and asks claude opus 5 to put them back. on free verse with rhyme, metre and line-initial capitals removed, it recovers 70 percent of the breaks that strand a determiner or a conjunction from its host, where a grammar-and-line-length solver recovers none. asked to write free verse of its own, it produces those breaks at 6.1 percent against the human 12.3. it locates the poet's override of the sentence and does not perform one.

the dead ear: a language model's inherited accent is an archive, not an author

2026 · sole author · accepted, neurips 2026 creative ai · pdf · openreview · code

pope rhymed tea with obey. shakespeare rhymed prove with love. both were sounds before they were conventions, and a model that learned rhyme from five centuries of verse learned it in accents nobody speaks. six documented sound changes give 997 dead pairs, crossed against a spelling confound. opus 5 treats dead rhymes as rhyme-like at 40.0 percent against a 1.2 percent floor, and writes them at 0.6 percent, below the most modern half-century in the corpus. the inheritance is a reference library, consulted accurately and kept out of the writing.

when does a fourier neural operator fail? spectral diagnostics for low-rank surrogates of reaction-diffusion

2026 · first author, with david j. zhang, wenhao lu, jonathan liu and spursh deshpande · accepted to two neurips 2026 workshops: representations for the physical sciences, and physical understanding for decision-making (poster) · pdf

neural surrogates for pde simulators are judged by one-step error, which hides the failures that matter: error piling up under rollout, and collapse on unseen regimes. on gray-scott, a compact fourier operator's one-step edge and its single-seed rollout advantage both reverse under three-seed averaging. rollout error follows the fate of the high-frequency spectrum, which the cnn over-damps and the operator over-excites. the argument is for spectral diagnostics computed from simulator output alone, before any model is trained.

[re] a novel cnn ablation reveals limited fourier inductive bias in litefno: a reproducibility study

2026 · with jacob goldstein, kristen zhou, wenhao lu, david j. zhang, spursh deshpande and jayvant rajesh · accepted, rescience c · pdf · doi · code

a from-scratch reproduction of the lightweight fourier neural operator, plus a parameter-matched low-rank cnn ablation the original paper does not include. on gray-scott at 32x32 across three seeds the cnn baseline matches or beats litefno on one-step error and on autoregressive rollout, so the study finds no consistent evidence for a fourier inductive-bias advantage at this scale.

erasure: a stated goal reorganises what a language model deletes

2026 · sole author · pdf · code

give a model a passage and a single operator, delete. change one word of the instruction. asked for a summary, opus 5 keeps proper nouns at +0.70 log-odds. asked for a poem, it cuts them at -0.73. random deletion sits at +0.08. the words that go to zero survival are the ones that say which thing this is (president, north, pursuant). the words that survive are home, wildlife, shadow, water, hope. run the same design on gpt-oss-120b and half of it reverses.

compulsory hope: instruction tuning teaches poems to evade, not to console

2026 · sole author · pdf · code

hardy's "hap" argues that suffering is meaningless chance. handed its opening, olmo 2 1b instruct finishes it with a guardian angel and eternal peace with god. its own base checkpoint, given a byte-identical prompt, does not. the paper measures that gap across checkpoints that differ only in how much preference training they received, with an ending-uplift metric built for poems.

the unchosen word: language models as editors of poetic indeterminacy

2026 · with wenhao lu · pdf · code

specscope: what did the operator learn?

2026 · extension of the litefno reproduction · code and notes

treats a trained fourier neural operator's spectral weights as an empirical transfer function of the system rather than as opaque parameters, and pulls out their pole structure with classical system identification. two questions: does that readout predict where the surrogate fails, and do the learned factors transplant across regimes? the testbed is an exactly solvable oscillatory pde, so the per-mode poles are known in closed form and "what the network should have learned" is not a matter of opinion.

mathematics

harmonic analysis

a half-constant upper bound for chowla’s cosine problem

2026 · co-first author · arxiv preprint forthcoming

chowla asked in 1965 how deep the negative dip of a cosine sum with n frequencies has to be. call the least possible depth λ(n). the best known lower bound is of order n1/5 (bedert, 2025), and the classical sidon-difference construction gives the upper bound (1+o(1))√(n/2). this paper lowers the leading constant to 1/2: λ(n) ≤ ½√n + O(n1/4√log n). the construction combines a two-sided sidon square identity with a singer difference set, a fejér-kernel majorant, a parity-biased deletion and an exact-cardinality random selection. in the other direction it carries the l1 norm through bedert’s estimate, giving a lower bound of order n1/4 for sets inside [1, cn] and one that recovers the exponent 1/5 and improves it when the l1 norm is small.

discrete geometry

isosceles sets in euclidean spaces of dimension nine to twenty-one

september 2026 · sole author · preprint · doi

a finite set is isosceles if every three of its points form an isosceles triangle. erdős asked in 1946 for the largest such set in dimension d, and the answer was known only up to d = 8. this proves iso(d) = (d+1 choose 2) + 1 for 9 ≤ d ≤ 21, with the unique extremal set the edge-midpoints of a regular simplex plus its centroid, and adds thirteen terms to oeis a175769. the proof runs blokhuis' decomposition into orthogonal two-distance sets (with a short new proof via gallai colourings) against the recent determination of maximum two-distance sets and musin's bound.

uniqueness of the two-distance extension of the 276 equiangular lines in r23

september 2026 · sole author · preprint · doi

ge, koolen and munemasa built a 277-point two-distance set in r23 by adding one point to representatives of the 276 equiangular lines, and asked whether a set of that size is unique. in the leech lattice model every extension point is a norm-6 lattice vector; there are exactly 257,600 of them and co3 × 2 permutes them transitively, so every such 277-point set is congruent to theirs. the automorphism group is 35:(2 × m11), and maximality gets a two-line proof that previously needed a large magma computation.
