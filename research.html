restored lines: a model finds the poet's hardest line breaks and declines to write them

2026 · sole author · accepted, neurips 2026 creative ai · pdf · openreview · code

a line break is the smallest unit of poetic decision. nothing in the sentence about the red wheelbarrow requires anyone to break it after "wheel". williams did that. this strips the breaks from 2,059 public-domain poems, grades every break by how much dependency structure it severs, and asks claude opus 5 to put them back. on free verse with rhyme, metre and line-initial capitals removed, it recovers 70 percent of the breaks that strand a determiner or a conjunction from its host, where a grammar-and-line-length solver recovers none. asked to write free verse of its own, it produces those breaks at 6.1 percent against the human 12.3. it locates the poet's override of the sentence and does not perform one.

the dead ear: a language model's inherited accent is an archive, not an author

2026 · sole author · accepted, neurips 2026 creative ai · pdf · openreview · code

pope rhymed tea with obey. shakespeare rhymed prove with love. both were sounds before they were conventions, and a model that learned rhyme from five centuries of verse learned it in accents nobody speaks. six documented sound changes give 997 dead pairs, crossed against a spelling confound. opus 5 treats dead rhymes as rhyme-like at 40.0 percent against a 1.2 percent floor, and writes them at 0.6 percent, below the most modern half-century in the corpus. the inheritance is a reference library, consulted accurately and kept out of the writing.

when does a fourier neural operator fail? spectral diagnostics for low-rank surrogates of reaction-diffusion

2026 · first author, with david j. zhang, wenhao lu, jonathan liu and spursh deshpande · accepted to two neurips 2026 workshops: representations for the physical sciences, and physical understanding for decision-making (poster) · pdf

neural surrogates for pde simulators are judged by one-step error, which hides the failures that matter: error piling up under rollout, and collapse on unseen regimes. on gray-scott, a compact fourier operator's one-step edge and its single-seed rollout advantage both reverse under three-seed averaging. rollout error follows the fate of the high-frequency spectrum, which the cnn over-damps and the operator over-excites. the argument is for spectral diagnostics computed from simulator output alone, before any model is trained.

[re] a novel cnn ablation reveals limited fourier inductive bias in litefno: a reproducibility study

2026 · with jacob goldstein, kristen zhou, wenhao lu, david j. zhang, spursh deshpande and jayvant rajesh · accepted, rescience c · pdf · doi · code

a from-scratch reproduction of the lightweight fourier neural operator, plus a parameter-matched low-rank cnn ablation the original paper does not include. on gray-scott at 32x32 across three seeds the cnn baseline matches or beats litefno on one-step error and on autoregressive rollout, so the study finds no consistent evidence for a fourier inductive-bias advantage at this scale.

solved

isosceles sets in euclidean spaces of dimension nine to twenty-one

solved · erdős, 1946 september 2026 · sole author · preprint · doi

a finite set is isosceles if every three of its points form an isosceles triangle. erdős asked in 1946 for the largest such set in dimension d, and the answer was known only up to d = 8. this proves iso(d) = (d+1 choose 2) + 1 for 9 ≤ d ≤ 21, with the unique extremal set the edge-midpoints of a regular simplex plus its centroid, and adds thirteen terms to oeis a175769. the proof runs blokhuis' decomposition into orthogonal two-distance sets (with a short new proof via gallai colourings) against the recent determination of maximum two-distance sets and musin's bound.

the 3-diametral number of euclidean d-space is 2d+2

solved october 2026 · sole author · doi

a configuration of points is 3-diametral if among any three of them two are at distance equal to the diameter. bezdek and lángi proved the largest such configuration has 2d+2 points for d ≤ 3 and asked whether 2d+2 holds in every dimension. this proves that it does, and that a regular simplex with every vertex taken twice is the only extremal configuration. the proof reads a configuration as the gram matrix of unit vectors in a lorentzian space and combines a trace identity with perron-frobenius. a construction for distinct points also shows polyanskii’s conjectured bound fails for every d ≥ 4 except 7.

four runners that are barely lonely

solved · fan and sun, conjecture 4.1 october 2026 · with karam gill and bryan zachary bu · preprint · doi

in the lonely runner problem, ml(v) is the largest loneliness any time t achieves for speeds v, and kravitz asked which values below 1/n occur. for four speeds, jain and kravitz showed the values below 1/4 are m/(4m+2) up to finitely many undetermined exceptions. this settles the four-runner case completely: a primitive quadruple has ml < 1/4 exactly when it is {1,2,3,4k}, or {x,y,x+y,x+2y} with gcd(x,y) = 1 and y ≢ x, 2x mod 4, or {1,3,4,14}. the values below 1/4 are therefore every m/(4m+2) with m ≥ 2 except 3/14, which never occurs, and conjecture 4.1 of fan and sun holds. a density lemma bounds the length of a quadruple on neither critical subtorus by 512, and an exact search over 800,816,099 quadruples finishes the proof. the same method shows the accumulation points of the four-runner spectrum in (0, 2/5] are the three-runner values there, as giri and kravitz conjectured, and for every n gives ml(v) > 1/n − (n(n−1))n−2/(2|v|), conditional on the conjecture for n−1 and n−2 speeds.

the orchard-planting problem for fifteen points

solved · open since 1974 september 2026 · with bryan zachary bu and karam gill · accepted, jmm 2027 (ams contributed paper session on combinatorics) · doi

proves t3(15) = 31, the largest number of three-point lines through fifteen points. it has been known since 1974 that t3(15) is 31 or 32, and oeis still lists it that way (a003035), so 15 is not a sporadic exception to the burr-grünbaum-sloane formula. the proof enumerates 7,955,290 isomorphism classes of partial steiner triple systems and shows none is realisable in the real projective plane, using pappus and desargues as filters and exact gröbner-basis computations for the rest, cross-validated by a sat encoding of oriented matroids. it also confirms t3(13) = 22 and t3(14) = 26, which had only been announced from an unpublished computation.

fifteen points determine at least nine ordinary lines

solved september 2026 · with bryan zachary bu and karam gill · doi

proves m(15) = 9 for the sylvester-gallai ordinary lines problem. only 7 ≤ m(15) ≤ 9 was known, the lower bound being the kelly-moser bound of 1958, and it was the first unknown entry of oeis a003034. the proof classifies 23,174,731 incidence structures and shows none is realisable over the reals, with every algebraic rejection confirmed by an independent sat test. one system turns out to be realisable over q(√−3) but not over the reals. it follows that the green-tao formula holds for every 6 ≤ n ≤ 16 except 13.

maximum two-distance sets in dimensions nine, ten, eleven and twelve

solved · open since 1997 september 2026 · with bryan zachary bu and karam gill · doi

proves g(9) = 45, open since lisoněk’s 1997 work left 45 ≤ g(9) ≤ 55, and shows that up to similarity there are exactly two 45-point two-distance sets in r9. it also proves g(10) = 55, g(11) = 66 and g(12) = 78, each attained only by the simplex-midpoint set. the method is a root-lattice reduction plus a symmetry-reduced exhaustive search, cross-checked by an independent sat-based implementation. my isosceles-sets paper builds on this result.

maximum two-distance sets in euclidean spaces of dimension up to twenty-one

solved september 2026 · joint with bryan zachary bu and yu’s group · doi

proves g(d) = (d+1 choose 2) for every 13 ≤ d ≤ 21, with the simplex-midpoint set the only extremal set, and reproves d = 10 to 12 without the computer-assisted clique searches used there. the searches are replaced by a combinatorial argument: in the lattice an the problem becomes a uniform set family with two intersection sizes, handled by ray-chaudhuri-wilson and ahlswede-khachatrian, and in dn it becomes a binary code bounded by barg and musin. my isosceles-sets paper also uses this result.

uniqueness of the two-distance extension of the 276 equiangular lines in r23

answers an open question september 2026 · sole author · preprint · doi

ge, koolen and munemasa built a 277-point two-distance set in r23 by adding one point to representatives of the 276 equiangular lines, and asked whether a set of that size is unique. in the leech lattice model every extension point is a norm-6 lattice vector; there are exactly 257,600 of them and co3 × 2 permutes them transitively, so every such 277-point set is congruent to theirs. the automorphism group is 35:(2 × m11), and maximality gets a two-line proof that previously needed a large magma computation.

largest admissible digit sets modulo primes up to 31

solved september 2026 · doi

elsholtz, klahn and lipnik build large progression-free sets from admissible digit sets and tabulated the largest they could find for primes up to 31; seventeen entries were recorded only as lower bounds. this determines all seventeen. two increase, modulo 31 to 23 for k = 5 and 24 for k = 6, and the other fifteen are optimal. for three-term progressions the largest admissible digit set modulo any odd prime p has exactly (p+1)/2 elements. the key observation is that admissibility is a linear program, so gordan’s theorem gives short certificates checkable in exact integer arithmetic.

a parity obstruction for neumaier graphs with e = s − 2

answers an open question september 2026 · with bryan zachary bu and karam gill · accepted, jmm 2027 (ams contributed paper session on graph theory) · doi

proves that if μ is odd then 4 divides s, which explains why these parameter sets vanish in the computations of abiad, de boeck and zeijlemaker and answers their question. it also eliminates five further open parameter sets from their table.

new records

a half-constant upper bound for chowla’s cosine problem

new record october 2026 · co-first author, with bryan bu, fayzan khan, atharv jha, vihaan vajpeyi, ishaan chandupatla and madhav nagpal · arxiv:2610.17160

chowla asked in 1965 how deep the negative dip of a cosine sum with n frequencies has to be. call the least possible depth λ(n). the best known lower bound is of order n1/5 (bedert, 2025), and the classical sidon-difference construction gives the upper bound (1+o(1))√(n/2). this paper lowers the leading constant to 1/2: λ(n) ≤ ½√n + O(n1/4√log n). the construction combines a two-sided sidon square identity with a singer difference set, a fejér-kernel majorant, a parity-biased deletion and an exact-cardinality random selection. in the other direction it carries the l1 norm through bedert’s estimate, giving a lower bound of order n1/4 for sets inside [1, cn] and one that recovers the exponent 1/5 and improves it when the l1 norm is small.

bounds for k-diametral point configurations in euclidean space

new record october 2026 · sole author · doi

the sequel to the 3-diametral paper. bezdek and lángi’s upper bound for k-diametral configurations is exponential in d; this proves fk(ed) ≤ 2(d+1)k−2 and the subquadratic f4(ed) ≤ 15d3/2, by making the lorentzian gram matrix positive semidefinite with a low-rank correction. it also shows the conjectured equality fk = (k−1)(d+1) fails already for k = 6, using two-distance sets of bondarenko and of jenrich and brouwer, and reduces the k = 4 case to an explicit inequality.

an improved lower bound in the nonlinear roth theorem

new record september 2026 · with bryan zachary bu and karam gill · doi

builds subsets of {1, …, n} of size n0.77509… with no configuration {x, x+y, x+y2}, improving younis’s exponent 0.76850, by running his iterated-difference framework at modulus 145. it also solves the optimisation exactly, with big-integer certificates, at moduli 65 and 85.

almost-equidistant sets and triangle-free graphs

new record september 2026 · with bryan zachary bu and karam gill · accepted, jmm 2027, presented by me (ams contributed paper session on graph theory) · doi

the pairs at non-unit distance in an almost-equidistant set form a triangle-free graph, and this studies f(d), the largest such set in rd, through that graph in both directions. constructions from the hoffman-singleton, gewirtz, m22 and higman-sims graphs give f(10) ≥ 26 and f(22) ≥ 100, and beat the previous record 2d + 4 for every 20 ≤ d ≤ 57; the method gives nothing beyond d = 57. on the upper-bound side it proves rank(a − λ2i) > n/(8(λ2+1)) for every triangle-free graph, so the conjecture f(d) = O(d) holds whenever the non-unit distances are bounded away from 1. a strongly regular graph meeting the krein bound with triangle-free parameters would give f(d) ≥ (1+o(1))d4/3 and make the kupavskii-mustafa-swanepoel bound sharp.

larger few-distance sets in r6, r7, r8 and r9 from shells of root lattices

new record september 2026 · with bryan zachary bu and karam gill · doi

szöllősi and östergård tabulated the largest known s-distance sets for d ≤ 8 and called the entries for s = 5, 6 in dimensions 6 to 8 “rather weak”. the vectors of norm at most 6 in e6, e7 and e8 contain much larger ones: 910 points in r8 for s = 6 against 504, and 388 in r7 against 280. every set is given as integer vectors and checked by an exact script.

an improved lower bound for sets with no cube differences

new record september 2026 · doi

ruzsa’s construction turns a subset of z/mz with no nonzero cube difference into a large subset of {1, …, n} with the same property. the best exponent recorded was 0.861681, from lewko’s 14-element set modulo 91. an explicit 116-element set modulo 3367 = 7 × 13 × 37 raises it to 0.861763. the modulus came from a structural analysis that rules out every modulus 7p with p ≤ 163 and singles out 3367, and the set itself from a tabu search; every finite claim is checked by a standard-library script.

lower bound for sets lacking {x, x+y, x+y3}

new record september 2026 · doi

the first explicit lower bound for sets lacking {x, x+y, x+y3} that goes beyond what sets without cube differences give: exponent 0.8703797… against 0.8617631…. the construction runs younis’s iterated-difference framework with a two-step chain of subsets of z/91z of sizes 13 and 28, and an exact dynamic program proves that no chain of any length modulo 91 does better.

improved lower bounds for sárközy’s theorem in function fields from independent sets in products of generalized paley graphs

new record september 2026 · doi

naslund built large sets of polynomials over fq with no two elements differing by a nonzero k-th power, with the size governed by the independence number rk,2(fq) of a generalised paley graph product. two independent exhaustive computations give r3,2(f13) = 21, raising the exponent for cube differences from naslund’s 0.863882 to 0.864495, the largest this method has produced. verified independent sets also give exponents 0.913841 for fourth powers over f17 (previously 0.875) and 0.961815 for seventh powers over f29 (previously 0.928571), and there is an infinite family of exactly computable cases from subfields.

selection consumes diversity: a closed-form ceiling on self-improvement from verifier-filtered data

fsml 2026 · under review

rejection-sampling fine-tuning, star-style bootstrapping and self-rewarding models share one loop: sample, keep what a verifier scores highly, refit. each round is truncation selection, so the breeder’s equation applies. with a noiseless verifier the total gain over all rounds is finite and closed-form, and the gain being linear in the remaining spread is exactly the entropy-performance law reported for reinforcement learning of reasoning models. on real image data with a learned reward model, one pre-loop measurement predicts the total gain: 9.69 predicted, 9.74 measured.

what do self-judged scores measure? an audit of reciprocal evaluation in unified multimodal models

wacv 2027, unimug workshop · under review

a unified multimodal model can grade its own images: it generates an image, answers multiple-choice questions about it, and the accuracy becomes its score, as in unieval’s uniscore. the paper shows that the published scores cannot identify the “understanding part” they are used to report, and gives sharp bounds on it. an audit of the released benchmark finds that a reader that always answers “a” scores 0.351, because the gold letters are skewed and never shuffled, and that one model’s self-judged scores equal that reader’s in 77 of 81 tags. it also proves that cross-reading among four or more models identifies each reader’s self-excess.

counting ink: what an autism scanpath-image benchmark measures

wacv 2027, vision4neuro workshop · under review

a public set of 547 eye-tracking scanpath images from 54 children has become a benchmark for image-based autism screening, with reported accuracies up to 99.8%. under participant-grouped cross-validation, a score with no parameters, the share of non-black pixels, separates autistic from typically developing children with auc 0.85, and probes on four imagenet backbones add little to it. the images clip each trace at a fixed axes box, which 69.4% of autistic children’s traces reach against 40.2% of the others. the benchmark mostly measures how much of a recording is lost, noisy or off screen, not where children look.

what a clothing description reveals: singling out, linkability and inference in pedestrian attribute search under eu law

wacv 2027, rws workshop · under review

the eu ai act treats sex, age and hair colour as biometric categories, so a natural fix for attribute-based person search is to keep clothing only. this turns the three anonymisation tests of european data protection guidance into statistics and runs them on 224,589 annotated images. clothing alone singles out 59.0% of people in random groups of 100, links a person across market-1501 cameras at rank one in 38.9% of cases against 0.09% chance, and predicts the annotated sex label with auc 0.67 to 0.79 on held-out datasets. each measurement is mapped to the gdpr and ai act provisions it informs.

lost before any attack: auditing the invisible watermark shipped with sdxl and flux.1

wacv 2027, signal workshop · under review

the reference code of stable diffusion xl and flux.1 marks every output with an open-source invisible watermark, and stability ai distributes a detector for it. auditing the shipped chain from embedder to detector shows most marks are unreadable before any processing: the embedder and detector disagree on a colour offset (0.5 against 128), and flux.1’s documented 48-bit message has 46 bits. the official detector flags only 45.5% of sdxl-generated images as watermarked. a one-line detector change raises that to 90.9%, and a repaired embedder reaches 100%.

sharper is not truer: super-resolution rewrites license plates before they are read

wacv 2027, rws workshop · under review

super-resolution is the usual proposal for plates captured too small to read. across six general-purpose upscalers and two open plate readers on the openalpr benchmark, only one of 48 comparisons with plain bicubic interpolation read more plates with confidence, by 2.6 points, and gan training cost 10 to 28 points. real-esrgan changed the read of 58% of plates and broke half the reads bicubic got right. the paper proves no upscaler can raise the bayes accuracy of plate reading, and two diffusion runs on the same capture disagreed on 40% of plates. voting over five captured frames helped where upscaling hurt.

slow walkers count more: how the eth/ucy test protocol weights pedestrians

wacv 2027, urbanstate workshop · under review

pedestrian forecasting is still ranked on eth/ucy with a loader that cuts recordings into overlapping 8-second windows. the reported error is a weighted mean in which each pedestrian counts once per window they fill, so slow walkers dominate and anyone who crosses in under 8 seconds counts zero: 39% of pedestrians are never evaluated, and one who barely moves supplies 9.7% of the zara2 test samples. counting each pedestrian once raises average error by 10 to 57% and reverses one published model ranking, and confidence intervals are 2.3 to 8.1 times too narrow.

the camera moved: a violence detection benchmark separated by its recording sessions

wacv 2027, rws workshop · under review

violence detectors are compared on small benchmarks with reported accuracies above 95%. on airtlab the published split puts chunks of every test clip in training, and a nearest-neighbour rule on 36 × 64 grey thumbnails scores 99.3%, above every published model. much of what remains comes from the recording session: pasting each test clip’s moving people onto a background of the other class drops balanced accuracy from 86.1% to 43.1%, below chance.

no image needed: answer priors and a lenient scorer in a sar vision-language benchmark

wacv 2027, sense workshop · under review

auditing the question-answering part of sarlang-1m without opening a single image: answering each question with the most common training answer for its template scores 65.0% under strict exact match. the released scorer counts a prediction as correct whenever the reference appears anywhere inside it, so the string “yes | no” scores 42.7% and one 965-character string scores 100.0% on 99,522 test questions. two more remote-sensing scorers share the substring rule, and three give an empty answer full marks.

half the test ships are in the training crops: auditing the hrsid split for sar ship detection

wacv 2027, sense workshop · under review

hrsid splits overlapping crops of 136 source images rather than the images themselves. 94.8% of test crops share pixels with a training crop and 53.5% of test ships lie entirely inside one, so pasting training boxes into the test crops, with no detector at all, scores 42.4 coco ap. the overlap strips act as a natural experiment: a detector recalls 3.8 points more of the test ships it saw in training.

is the synergy above the noise? noise floors for understanding-generation claims in unified multimodal models

wacv 2027, unimug workshop · under review

unified multimodal models are justified by synergy between generating and understanding images, usually from ablations run once. seed variance turns out to be a stable multiple of binomial variance, so every accuracy-type benchmark has a noise floor computable from its size and score. of 261 results from 28 papers, 42% lie within their floor, claimed benefits clear it less often than claimed harms, and no paper reports seed variation. the paper gives minimum detectable differences for 25 benchmarks.

seen scenes, blind answers: an audit of the reva drone video qa benchmark

wacv 2027, mavu workshop · under review

reva’s paper states its splits are disjoint by video sequence. the public annotation files show 1,012 of 1,014 test clips also appear in training, and the validation file publishes the answers of 392 test items. copying labelled answers plus a text-only classifier scores 90.5% on validation without a single pixel, and on genuinely held-out recordings the text-only classifier still scores 58.1% against 25% chance.

holds as free probes: timing-aware temporal evaluation of anime video

wacv 2027, ainime workshop · under review

anime holds each drawing for several frames, and temporal metrics built for live action cannot tell a held drawing from a stable one: re-exposing motion on fours raises vbench’s motion smoothness although three quarters of the drawings are gone. holds are also useful, since any change a restoration model makes inside a held region is its own flicker. that measure needs no reference, and it shows the recurrent animesr changing by 0.81 levels on bit-identical input against at most 0.16 for per-frame models.

retailaction without pixels: metadata baselines and metric checks

wacv 2027, praw workshop · under review

every retailaction sample, including the hidden challenge split, ships with metadata from the provider’s tracking pipeline. a three-layer transformer that reads only that metadata scores 71.9 map, against 41.7 for the best published video model, and the face track alone scores 52.2. the paper also finds the temporal labels misdescribed in the dataset card and a contact-point rule that adds 11.2 points.

capped at 100: per-image detection limits and the comparability of sku-110k scores

wacv 2027, praw workshop · under review

sku-110k is scored with coco-style average precision, which keeps 100 detections per image by default, yet 95.9% of its test images hold more than 100 products. from the annotations alone, no detector can exceed 0.675 recall or 0.673 ap under that cap. common toolchains apply the cap inconsistently, so the same detections from one detector score anywhere from 0.326 to 0.470 ap depending on the convention, and the cap even reverses which nms threshold looks best. only 3 of 10 published results state the cap.

sharper is not closer: what no-reference metrics reward in anime restoration

wacv 2027, ainime workshop · under review

real degraded anime has no clean reference, so restoration is ranked with no-reference quality metrics. degrading 40 frames so each source serves as its own reference shows what they reward: maniqa prefers a sharpened copy of the source to the source itself in 90% of clips, and one unsharp mask that lowers psnr in every clip is preferred by four learned metrics in every clip. maniqa and hyperiqa even rank one restoration above its own source frame in every clip.

same plate, both splits: train-test leakage in nutrition5k and what it does to food foundation-model probes

wacv 2027, fabric workshop · under review

nutrition5k’s authors state that no plate appears in both splits. the scan times and weighed ingredient masses it already ships reveal 4,211 pairs of scans of the same food, against 8.1 expected by chance, and 9.0% of the side-view test dishes are repeat scans of training food with identical labels. a frozen clip nearest-neighbour probe retrieves the training repeat for 57 of 63 leaked dishes, and the leak cuts its error from 92.4 to 81.0 kcal.

the drone has been here before: shared places between the training and test splits of visdrone

wacv 2027, rws workshop · under review

visdrone2019-det’s authors state its splits were captured at different locations. homographies fitted to matched features show 16.8% of validation images, and about a tenth of test frames, film a place seen in training, because the splits interleave along the video numbering. the shared content is the place rather than the objects, and a detector gains nothing measurable from it: removing the seen places costs seen and unseen validation images equally.

one in how many? false alert rates of live facial recognition in london, from controlled test to routine use

wacv 2027, rws workshop · under review

the metropolitan police cite a controlled test putting false positives at about 1 in 6,000 passers. transcribing all 729 routine deployments from 2023 to 2026, 10.1 million recorded faces and 58 false alerts, shows the denominator is unstable: recorded faces per hour at the same sites differ elevenfold between periods. the records cannot bound the rate at the level the test predicts, and detecting a twofold demographic difference would take 6 to 8 years of deployments.

fifty milliseconds late: latency and saccades in event-based eye-tracking benchmarks

wacv 2027, eof workshop · under review

event cameras are proposed for eye tracking on smart glasses because they follow fast eye movements with little delay, yet the benchmarks score only spatial error at labelled instants. on the 3et+ 2024 challenge labels, a tracker with no spatial error that reports every position 50 ms late scores 1.25 to 1.35 pixels, close to the best entry’s 1.44. the metrics cannot tell latency from spatial error, and they dilute it: saccades fill 7.9% of the time but carry 56% of the error that 10 ms of lag causes. the paper proposes a latency-aware protocol.

when retries repeat mistakes: verification and rollback for small-model agents

oe-agent 2026 · under review

small open models can run agents on two cpu cores, but over long tasks their step errors compound. the usual fix verifies every k steps, rolls back on a flag and retries. the paper gives the exact success probability and cost of that loop, and proves that acting on single flags stretches the reachable horizon by at most (1−f)/(1−d). small models break the assumption that a retry is a fresh draw: a resampled failed qwen2.5-1.5b step fails again with probability 0.63, though the mean error rate is 4.2%. retries by a different model fix 80% of flagged steps at the first retry instead of 33%. with a strong verifier, retry diversity rather than model size is the limit.

hertz is not reactivity: reaction latency of chunked robot policies under stochastic inference latency

corl 2026, eeai workshop · under review

speed results for robot foundation models are reported as frequencies, and none of them says how old the observation behind the executing action is. modelling chunked execution under random inference latency as a renewal process gives the mean reaction latency in closed form, μ + e[t2]/(2e[t]). under heavy-tailed latency, delaying each inference start to a computable threshold cuts reaction latency by 13 to 20% at no cost in motion. across 88 simulated configurations, predicted reaction latency ranks tracking error with spearman ρ = 0.98, against 0.84 and 0.75 for the two frequencies people usually report.

seconds to spare: interception slack for fair and graded robot tennis benchmarks

roboletics 2026 · under review

sport benchmarks for human-robot interaction compare bodies that differ in speed, reach and latency, and a raw success rate mixes what a body allows with what its controller achieves. interception slack, the spare time with which a body could reach the ball, gives an exact feasibility ceiling, and factoring success into ceiling and efficiency reverses raw rankings. slack marks 12 to 67% of shots as impossible for a given body, and wheelchair tennis’s two-bounce rule is worth 0.05 to 0.13 s against a gap of at least 0.88 s to an able-bodied player.

the cdc influenza forecast ensemble predicts changes that are too small

september 2026 · sole author · submitted to the columbia junior science journal · code and data

the cdc combines weekly hospital-admission forecasts from many teams by taking their median. across 35,162 ensemble forecasts from the flusight, covid-19 and rsv forecast hubs for 2023 to 2026, regressing the change that happened on the change predicted gives a slope of 1.65 (95% ci 1.29 to 1.94) for influenza four weeks ahead, above 1 in all 52 jurisdictions: the ensemble’s predicted changes are too small. most individual team models show the opposite, and the median strips out their disagreement and leaves a shared change that is too small. rescaling by the slope fitted on earlier seasons improved the hubs’ standard accuracy score by 2% and 9% in the two later seasons.
